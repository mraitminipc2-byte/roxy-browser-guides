Proxies for Antidetect Browsers: A Practical Guide

A reference guide based on MR AIT’s antidetect browser course

Profiles isolate everything except one thing by default: your IP address. This guide covers the proxy half of the workflow — what to use, and how to know it’s good.

The one-IP-per-profile rule

Five profiles on one home IP look related no matter how good their fingerprints are. One dedicated IP per profile is the baseline; sharing a proxy across profiles reintroduces the correlation you paid to remove.

Know the proxy types

  • Datacenter proxies — cheap and fast, from servers. Fine for platforms that don’t scrutinize IP quality; heavily-used sites flag known datacenter ranges.
  • Residential proxies — IPs from real consumer connections. More expensive, far more believable — the usual choice for accounts that matter.
  • Mobile proxies — carrier IPs, the most trusted and the most expensive; used for the most sensitive platforms.

Start testing with a free proxy trial so you learn what reliability, geo-targeting, and speed feel like before committing money.

The pre-trust checklist

  1. Connectivity — attach it to a test profile; confirm the reported IP and country.
  2. Stability — leave a session open; a proxy that drops mid-session is worse than no proxy.
  3. Geo match — the IP’s country should match the profile’s timezone and language (the mismatch lesson from Step 6).
  4. Speed — usable for real work, not just a passing ping.
The mistake that costs accounts: buying profiles and fingerprints but skipping proxy quality. The course repeats it in every video — quality proxies are where multi-account setups live or die.

Where do proxies plug in?

In the antidetect browser, per profile — see the Roxy Browser walkthrough for the exact flow: add the proxy, test it, then launch the profile on it.
