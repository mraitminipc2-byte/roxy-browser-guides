Create an iPhone Device Profile (Course Step 6)

Based on the video "antidetect browser course: create an iPhone device (Step 6)" by MR AIT · watch on YouTube

Step 6 goes mobile: creating an iPhone-like device profile inside your antidetect browser for ethical testing, mobile UX checks, and geo/QA workflows. Mobile is where most traffic lives, so testing as a mobile device is a core skill.

What makes mobile fingerprints different

A believable iPhone profile lines up several fields at once — if they disagree, the profile falls apart:

  • User agent — must declare iOS and Safari, matching the iPhone model you choose.
  • Viewport & screen — the device's real resolution and device pixel ratio (DPR).
  • WebGL & graphics data — should match what that iPhone hardware reports.
  • Sensors & platform quirks — touch-first behavior and iOS-specific details.

The video walks through selecting an iPhone version (15/16 and beyond) and configuring these settings so they tell one consistent story.

Align proxy, timezone, and language

The fingerprint alone isn't the whole story. Your profile's timezone, language, and locale must match the proxy's country — an iPhone in Toronto shouldn't greet sites in a different language from a Frankfurt IP. The video shows how to align these to avoid the obvious mismatches that flag a profile in lawful testing scenarios.

Validate the mobile profile

Don't assume it works — check it. The lesson's QA method:

  1. Open a public fingerprint test page from inside the profile.
  2. Confirm the reported device, OS, screen, and timezone all match what you configured.
  3. Work through a simple checklist: user agent, screen, timezone, locale, fonts.
Ethics note from the video: this course is for privacy education, QA testing, and legitimate marketing workflows. Do not use profiles to break laws, bypass platform controls, or commit fraud. Always follow site terms and local regulations.
